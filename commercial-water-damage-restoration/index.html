Commercial water damage restoration in Mineola, NY covers businesses, offices, shops, restaurants and multi-unit buildings that have had a leak, flood or sewage backup. Downtime costs money, so it is an emergency. Call the 24/7 line, shut off the water to the affected area if you can, move inventory and equipment off the floor and photograph the loss before cleanup.

Commercial losses involve more than water. Tenants, landlords, building managers and insurers all have a stake, and the documentation matters as much as the drying.

When a business water loss is an emergency

Treat it as urgent when water reaches inventory or electrical equipment, when it is spreading to a neighboring unit or when part of the space is unsafe to use. After hours, a leak in an empty building can run all night, so ask your property manager for the shut-off locations now.

Who is responsible in a commercial loss

Common roles in a commercial loss
Party. Usual responsibility. What to gather.
Tenant. Contents, inventory, build-out improvements. Photos of stock, equipment lists, lease sections.
Landlord or owner. Building structure and common areas. Building policy, maintenance records.
Property manager. Coordination and access. Contacts, floor plans, shut-off locations.
Insurer. Coverage decisions. Claim number, adjuster contact.

Read the lease. Some leases make the tenant responsible for cleanup of the space and others place it on the landlord. Ask your insurer which policy responds.

Mineola's mix of commercial space

Mineola has retail and office space along Jericho Turnpike and Old Country Road, medical and county campuses and apartment buildings near the LIRR station. Each type has its own water risks. A restaurant has grease traps and floor drains. An office has ceiling tile and carpet tile. A retail strip has shared walls, so one tenant's leak can wet the unit next door.

When you call, tell the dispatcher how many units are affected and which ones share walls, ceilings or floors with the loss.

Documentation for the owner and the insurer

  • Photographs of the damage, the source and the date and time.
  • A list of damaged inventory and equipment, with serial numbers where available.
  • Moisture readings and the equipment log from the drying crew.
  • Receipts for emergency repairs and temporary measures.
  • Contacts for the landlord, tenants and the adjuster.

Business interruption coverage, if you carry it, may require proof of lost income. Ask your insurer which records are needed and keep them from the first day.

What to ask before work starts

Ask who will oversee the job, how drying will be phased, what readings will be reported and how the scope and price will be explained. A clear scope in writing before work starts protects you and the building owner.

Commercial work follows the same IICRC S500 standard as residential work, with extra attention to documentation for property managers and insurers.

Before we arrive, close the building's water supply if you can find it, keep staff away from wet electrical equipment and photograph inventory.

Keeping the business open

Where possible, drying is phased so part of a space stays usable. Containment barriers and negative air control dust and odor. Flooring such as carpet tile can often be lifted and dried or replaced by section, which helps to limit downtime.

Sewage and flood losses in commercial space are handled under the same standards as at home, and the sewage backup cleanup and flood damage restoration pages explain those steps.

What affects commercial restoration costs

Commercial losses are priced by size and complexity.

  • Square footage and number of units.
  • Materials such as ceiling tile, carpet tile and drywall.
  • Phasing to keep the business open.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Rebuild and finish work.

Commercial policies differ in what they cover, including business interruption. Ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call (516) 845-9582 and a dispatcher will explain the scope and the price before work starts.
