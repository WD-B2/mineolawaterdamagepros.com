Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Mineola Homeowners.

Not all water is the same. The category tells you how contaminated the water is, and it changes how the cleanup works.

By the Mineola Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 9, 2026

Restoration companies talk about water categories because the source of the water decides what must be done. The IICRC S500 standard defines three categories, and the idea is simple. The more contaminated the water, the more careful and extensive the cleanup. Here is what each category means in practice.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source, such as a broken supply line, a leaking faucet or a failed water heater tank. It starts clean, but it does not stay that way. As water sits and touches materials, it can pick up contaminants and grow worse over time.

Cleanup of Category 1 water is the least invasive. Extraction and drying often save carpet, pad and drywall if the work begins quickly. See burst pipe water damage for a typical case.

Category 2: gray water

Category 2 water carries some contamination. Examples include overflow from a washing machine or dishwasher, a toilet overflow with urine only, or water from a sump pump failure. It may cause discomfort if people come into contact with it, so protective equipment is used.

Cleanup is more careful. Carpet pad and other porous materials are often removed. Carpet may be cleaned and dried in some cases. Surfaces are cleaned and sanitized. Category 1 water left too long can also become Category 2.

Category 3: black water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated. Sewage backups, rising floodwater from outside and standing water that has been wet for a long time all fall here. It can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so protective equipment and strict handling are required.

Porous materials touched by Category 3 water are usually removed and disposed of. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected. Do not wade into this water or try to handle it yourself. The sewage backup cleanup page lists the safety steps.

Water categories at a glance
Category. Typical source. Usual handling.
1. Supply line, faucet, water heater. Extract and dry. Many materials can be saved if the work starts fast..
2. Washer or dishwasher overflow, sump failure. Protective gear. Porous materials often removed. Surfaces cleaned..
3. Sewage, rising flood water, long-standing water. Strict protection. Porous materials removed. Disinfect hard surfaces..

Why this matters when you compare estimates

Two estimates for a similar-looking loss can differ because the companies assumed different categories. A company that treats the water as Category 1 will plan to dry carpet and pad. A company that treats it as Category 2 will plan to remove the pad. Neither is wrong without knowing the source and the elapsed time.

Ask each company what category it assumed and why. A good answer names the source and the time since the loss. A vague answer is a reason to ask more.

Categories also appear in documentation sent to insurers, so an accurate description of the source helps everyone. If you are not sure where the water came from, say so. The crew can usually work it out on site.

Why the category can change

Time and temperature matter. Clean water that sits for a day or two in a warm basement can become gray. Gray water left longer can become black. This is one more reason to start extraction the same day.

What you can do while you wait

  • Stay out of the water if it came from a drain or from outside.
  • Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash your hands afterward.
  • Keep children and pets away.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry.
  • Take photos from a distance before cleanup.

Insurance and categories

Coverage often follows the source. A sudden supply line failure is commonly covered. A sewer backup or a flood may be excluded unless you bought a rider or a separate policy. Check with your insurer, and describe the source accurately.

Homes in New Hyde Park and Williston Park have a mix of older and newer plumbing, so the sources vary from street to street. The category does not depend on the age of the house, only on the water.

Where mold fits

Mold is not a water category, but any category can lead to it if materials stay wet. Fast drying lowers the risk, as described in the structural drying guide.

A local note on drains and backups

Mineola homes connect to a local sewer system, and older lines can back up in heavy storms. If water comes up through a floor drain, treat it as Category 3 until a professional says otherwise.

For permits during repair, the Village of Mineola Building Department requires them for plumbing, electrical and construction work. Mineola became a village in 1906.

Questions about this topic in Mineola

Can Category 1 water become Category 3?

Yes, if it sits long enough. Time, temperature and the materials it touches can raise the category.

Is washing machine overflow Category 1?

It is usually treated as Category 2, because the water carries detergent and soil.

Can I keep carpet after Category 2 water?

Sometimes the carpet can be cleaned and dried while the pad is removed. It depends on the specifics.

Does Category 3 mean the house is a total loss?

No. It means porous materials are removed and hard surfaces are disinfected.

What should I do if a Mineola floor drain backs up?

Treat it as Category 3, stay out of the water and call a professional. Check your policy for backup coverage.
