Storm water damage cleanup in Mineola, NY covers water that gets into a building through the roof, windows, doors or walls during a storm, plus the cleanup after wind damage. If water is entering during or after a storm, call the 24/7 line, protect belongings and photograph the damage.

Storm damage is about the building envelope: what failed and how water got in. It differs from rising floodwater, which is covered under flood damage restoration.

When storm water is an emergency

Treat it as urgent when water is entering a living space, when a tree or limb has opened the roof or when power is out and a sump pump is stopped. Cover openings with tarps if you can do so safely, and do not climb on a wet roof.

After a storm

Stay away from downed power lines, call PSEG Long Island or 911 and do not touch wet electrical equipment. Do not enter a flooded lower level if water is near outlets or the panel.

How storms damage Mineola homes

Long Island gets nor'easters, summer thunderstorms and the remnants of tropical systems. The National Weather Service in Upton issued a flash flood warning on August 18, 2024 that named Mineola and several nearby places. Heavy rain, wind and fallen limbs open different doors for water.

Where storm water enters
Entry point. Typical cause. What to check.
Roof. Missing shingles, a fallen limb. Attic insulation, ceiling stains.
Windows and doors. Wind-driven rain, failed seals. Sills, trim and drywall below.
Basement. Overwhelmed drains or window wells. Floor, wall bottoms, stored items.
Gutters and downspouts. Clogs or overflow. Siding and foundation edge.

Protecting the property after the storm

Take photographs before you move anything. Cover roof or window openings with tarps or plastic if it is safe, and move valuables to dry rooms. Keep receipts for emergency measures, because the New York State Department of Financial Services says to protect property from further damage and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

Do not run generators indoors, and do not use a wet vacuum on a flooded basement if power is on in that area.

Power outages and sump pumps

A sump pump stops when the power goes out. If your home has one, a battery backup can keep it running, and checking it before storm season is worthwhile. If the pump has failed and water is rising, call the 24/7 line, and tell the dispatcher whether the power is off.

Storm water cleanup follows the IICRC S500 standard, which treats rain that has run across the ground or through a roof as more than clean water.

What the cleanup includes

The crew removes standing water, then checks for moisture in attic insulation, ceiling drywall, wall cavities and flooring. Wet insulation is pulled and drywall that stayed wet is cut out. Dehumidifiers and air movers run until readings return to normal. A roofer or contractor handles the repair to the opening.

After a major storm, many calls arrive at once, so response is limited by how many crews are available. Call early, and describe the situation clearly so the dispatcher can prioritize.

Questions about storm water damage in Mineola

Do you handle storm water emergencies at night or on weekends in Mineola?

Yes. Storms do not follow business hours, and the dispatch line is open around the clock. After large storms, demand is high, and a crew is sent as soon as one is available.

Is storm damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Wind and rain damage may be, while flood is usually excluded. Ask your insurer which applies.

Should I tarp the roof myself?

Only if you can do it safely from the ground or a stable surface. Avoid wet roofs. A roofer can tarp openings.

What should I photograph?

The openings, the wet rooms and damaged belongings, before cleanup.

Can you do emergency board-up?

We focus on water removal and drying. A roofer or contractor handles roof repair and board-up.
